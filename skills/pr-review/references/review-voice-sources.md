## Supported conclusions and limits

Across the usable histories and workplace chat, Randy repeatedly uses short,
direct questions and imperatives, contractions, concrete technical terms, and
explicit boundaries on scope, completeness, and verification. Informal starts
and occasional emphasis express cadence; incidental typos are not a style rule.
External administrative email is brief and purpose-first, but too sparse and
different in audience to set the PR-review register.

The professional register follows his explicit corrections: direct and concise,
without softened findings or unnecessary explanation, normal grammar, and no
profanity. His instruction to keep nits in an approval review body supersedes
older guidance allowing separate comments. Broad access and posting identity
do not make agent-drafted messages independent human voice evidence.

Confidence is strongest for directness, brevity, concrete claims, and explicit
evidence boundaries. Authored professional review/email samples are limited;
the examples in `review-voice.md` are synthesized, not claimed quotations.
Future explicit corrections or approved human-authored review samples should
refine this profile through the same shared reference.
